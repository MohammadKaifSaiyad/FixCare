## What went well
- **The decomposition instinct paid off twice.** Both "booking" and "B2 dispatch" were too big as
  documented; splitting (booking→7 slices; B2→B2a/B2b/B2c) kept each slice buildable *now* and avoided
  inventing speculative trust/location/cash fields. Surfacing the dependency gaps in brainstorming
  (before designing) was the right call each time.
- **The state-machine engine compounded well.** B1's `transitionBooking` (optimistic-locked + audited)
  carried B2a (actor gate) and B3 (the keystone) with small, additive extensions — no rework. The
  default-deny flip in B2a's review directly protected B3's keystone (a forgotten ARRIVED actor entry
  would've failed loud).
- **`/code-review` keeps finding real things the per-task agents miss** — the B2a default-allow gate and
  the B3 missing route-level `requireCustomerRole` were both caught there. Worth the two-pass cost on
  money/keystone paths.
- **Subagents flagged good deviations rather than hiding them** — e.g. B3's `no-code`→401-vs-409 refinement
  was a genuine improvement over my plan, raised explicitly for review.
